# Ratios and verdicts

A ratio means something only under one stated definition, for a business whose statements print its parts.

1. **Fix the formula before reading numbers** and write it in one sentence in the answer. Order of authority: the question's own words; else the company's printed definition or printed ratio (say where); else the standard definition, using the matching `calculate` function. Standard reading: gross profit subtracts every cost-of-sales line; free cash flow is operating cash flow less capital expenditure; debt means borrowings, short and long term, never total liabilities; a quick measure counts cash, short-term investments and receivables, and you say which other lines you added or left out.
2. **Check that it applies.** The statements must print the inputs (a split between current and non-current, a cost of sales line, an inventory line). A bank, insurer or similar business often does not. Then say the measure is not meaningful or not reported, and name the measure the company uses instead if it prints one. Do not build the input from lines that merely look similar.
3. **Check the inputs.** If a denominator is negative or zero, say a ratio would mislead and give the inputs. Use one definition, one basis (reported or adjusted) and one unit for every period compared. State the income period and the balance date when both are used.
4. **When two common definitions give different verdicts,** give the one you used and the other in a sentence.
5. **Commit.** For healthy, improving, consistent or similar questions, answer yes, no, improved or declined first, then the formula, the inputs and the result. If the question sets a threshold, measure it in the same terms (percentage points are not a percent change).
6. Compute with `calculate` from copied figures only, and name the function.
